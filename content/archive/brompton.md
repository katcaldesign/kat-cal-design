approach: |
  Research is only worth doing if it changes a decision, so I start from the decision rather than the method. I want to know what the business is about to commit to, and what we would need to believe to commit with any confidence.

  Business goals and user needs are the same question from two directions. If I only take one of them into a project, the findings don't get used.

processHeading: From question to handover
process:
  - title: Framing
    text: Naming the decision on the table, and what we would have to know to make it.
  - title: Method
    text: Choosing methods for the question and the time available, not out of habit.
  - title: Participants
    text: Recruiting for the edges of a group as well as the middle, where the design problems tend to sit.
  - title: Handover
    text: Turning findings into briefs, requirements and roadmaps other teams can pick up without me.

methodsHeading: Methods I use
methods:
  - title: 1:1 interviews
    text: The bulk of the work. Mostly with riders, sometimes with internal stakeholders and industry experts.
  - title: Stakeholder alignment
    text: Talking to the business early to learn which metrics a project will be judged on, before I design the study.
  - title: Long term product trials
    text: Product goes out for weeks at a time. The design job is making the data easy for someone to hand back.
  - title: Usability testing
    text: Watching people use the thing, which is still the fastest way to find where it fails.
  - title: Surveys and analysis
    text: For sizing an insight once qualitative work has told me what is worth asking about.
  - title: Co-creation workshops
    text: Useful early, when a concept is still moving and group dynamics surface things interviews miss.
  - title: Anthropometric research
    text: Percentile data for sizing decisions, plus recruiting at the upper and lower limits to test it.
  - title: Affinity diagramming
    text: How I consolidate qualitative research and work out what happens next.
